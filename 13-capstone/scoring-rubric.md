# 13 - Capstone Project: Scoring Rubric

How your capstone pitch is scored. Each criterion gets 1 to 4 points, for a total out of 24. Read it before you start your canvas, so you know what the judges look for.

---

| Criterion | 1: Not yet | 2: Developing | 3: Good | 4: Strong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem and value** | The problem isn't clear | The problem is clear, but not who it helps or how much | Clear problem, clear users, a rough idea of time saved | Clear problem and users, with time or errors saved worked out from the brief |
| **Workflow design** | Steps are missing or out of order | The steps work, but it's unclear which are AI, rules or people | Every step is marked AI, rule or person, with a clear trigger | As Good, and simple: nothing an AI does that a rule could do more reliably |
| **Human approval** | No approval step | A person approves at the end only | A person approves before anything leaves the company or money moves | As Good, and the design says what the approver sees and how they say no |
| **Risks and controls** | No risks named | Risks named, no controls | Each main risk has a control, including hidden instructions and personal data (Modules 11 and 12) | As Good, and the design handles the tricky cases in the brief's data |
| **Testing** | No test plan | Says it will be tested, not how | Test cases from the brief's data, with expected results (Module 07) | As Good, with a score to beat before the workflow is used |
| **Pitch** | Over time or hard to follow | Clear, but runs over or skips the canvas | Clear and on time, using the canvas | Clear, on time, and answers questions with the design, not guesses |

> **Tip:** the tricky cases are already in each brief's sample data. A team that tests its design on the data before pitching usually scores 3 or 4 on Risks and Testing.
